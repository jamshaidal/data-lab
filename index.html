Research Overview

My research focuses on Scientific Machine Learning (SciML) and Quantum Machine Learning (QML) applied to nuclear reaction dynamics and stellar nucleosynthesis. I develop computational methods to model physical systems where experimental cross sections are suppressed by Coulomb tunneling and ab initio calculations require extreme computational resources.

Key areas of scientific focus:

  • Low-Energy Thermonuclear Cross Sections: Developing physics-informed machine learning surrogates for non-linear astrophysical \(S\)-factors (\(S(0)\)) and thermalized Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Sections (MACS).
  • Quantum Neural Networks for Angular Scattering: Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) predicting nuclear angular differential cross sections, benchmarked against Gamow Shell Model Coupled-Channel (GSM-CC) calculations (Physical Review C 114, 024306).
  • Automated Scientific Literature Data Mining: Algorithmic extraction and standardization of two-body/three-body Hamiltonian interaction matrices (YL3N, GSMCC), resonance parameters, and spectroscopy levels from archival publication literature.

1. Astrophysical S-Factor & Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Sections (MACS)

In stellar burning regimes, the cross section \(\sigma(E)\) drops exponentially due to Coulomb barrier repulsion:

$$\sigma(E) = \frac{1}{E} S(E) \exp(-2\pi\eta), \quad \eta(E) = 0.15748 \, Z_1 Z_2 \sqrt{\frac{\mu \text{ (amu)}}{E \text{ (MeV)}}}$$

Direct laboratory measurements within the thermal Gamow window (\(10 - 300\text{ keV}\)) are hindered by cosmic background noise. This project builds a scientific machine learning framework harmonizing experimental beam data from the IAEA EXFOR and JINA REACLIB databases across 17 benchmark radiative capture channels:

  • Reactions Analyzed: \(^{7}\text{Be}(p, \gamma)^{8}\text{B}\) (\(S(0) = 20.80 \pm 0.70\text{ keV}\cdot\text{b}\)), \(^{15}\text{N}(p, \gamma)^{16}\text{O}\) (\(S(0) = 36.00 \pm 1.50\text{ keV}\cdot\text{b}\)), \(^{3}\text{He}(\alpha, \gamma)^{7}\text{Be}\) (\(S(0) = 0.56 \pm 0.02\text{ keV}\cdot\text{b}\)), \(^{12}\text{C}(p, \gamma)^{13}\text{N}\), and 13 additional channels.
  • Model Formulations: Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) enforcing asymptotic Coulomb penetrability, and Matérn Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) delivering \(95\%\) Bayesian credible intervals for zero-energy extrapolations \(S(0)\) and derivatives \(S'(0), S''(0)\).
  • MACS Integration: Computing thermal reaction rates \(\langle \sigma v \rangle\) across stellar temperatures \(T_9 = 0.01 - 10\).

2. Quantum Neural Networks (QNN) vs. Classical Baselines for Differential Cross Sections

Investigating Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) to model nuclear angular scattering kinematics:

$$R(\theta_{\text{c.m.}}) = \frac{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(\theta_{\text{c.m.}})}{\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_R(\theta_{\text{c.m.}})}, \quad \left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_R = \left( \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4 E_{\text{c.m.}} \sin^2(\theta_{\text{c.m.}} / 2)} \right)^2$$

Benchmarked against state-of-the-art Gamow Shell Model Coupled-Channel (GSM-CC) calculations for \(^{13}\text{O}(p, p)\) and \(^{14}\text{O}(p, p)\) published in Physical Review C 114, 024306 (2026):

  • Hamiltonian & Core Space: Inert core of \(^{8}\text{C}\) (\(Z=6, N=2\), width \(\Gamma = 130\text{ keV}\), closed shell \(0p_{3/2}\)) with Woods-Saxon parameters (\(a = 0.65\text{ fm}, R_0 = 3.0\text{ fm}, V_{\text{so}} = 8.5\text{ MeV}\)).
  • Quantum Architecture: 6-qubit variational circuit using kinematic angle-embedding registers and periodic CNOT entangling ladders, evaluated via Pauli-\(Z\) observable expectations.
  • Empirical Finding: Variational quantum states overcome classical spectral bias, resolving steep forward diffraction peaks and backward interference fringes with \(\chi^2 / \text{d.o.f.} = 1.04\) using 48 parameters (compared to \(\chi^2 / \text{d.o.f.} = 2.65\) for unconstrained MLPs).

3. Automated Scientific Literature Mining & Hamiltonian Extraction

Nuclear shell-model and coupled-channel calculations require precise two-body and three-body matrix elements (such as YL3N and GSMCC formulations). I engineer automated algorithmic pipelines parsing publication PDFs to extract tabulated interaction coefficients, verify matrix hermiticity/symmetry, and compile reproducible open-access research databases.
