Research Overview

My research focuses on Scientific Machine Learning (SciML), Quantum Neural Network (QNN) approximations, and high-throughput scientific data extraction. I investigate computational techniques to model complex physical phenomena where experimental data is sparse, noisy, or computationally prohibitive to calculate ab initio.

Primary domains of inquiry include:

  • Stellar Nucleosynthesis & Reaction Dynamics: Machine learning modeling of low-energy astrophysical S-factors and Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Sections (MACS).
  • Quantum Machine Learning for Scattering: Comparative expressivity of Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) versus deep classical architectures for nuclear angular differential cross sections (PRC benchmark).
  • Automated Scientific Literature Data Mining: Algorithmic extraction and semantic harmonization of Hamiltonian matrix elements, resonance parameters, and spectroscopy levels from unstandardized scientific publications.

Research Streams & Projects

1. Machine Learning for Astrophysical S-Factor & Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Sections (MACS)

Nuclear Astrophysics • Non-linear Regression • Data Harmonization

In stellar burning regimes, Coulomb repulsion makes cross-section measurements extremely difficult, necessitating accurate theoretical extrapolations to zero energy (\(S(0)\)). This project develops machine learning pipelines to reconstruct reaction rates and thermalized Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Sections (MACS) across isotopic reaction chains.

  • Constructed unified, multi-reaction databases harmonizing disparate experimental beam datasets with resonance Breit-Wigner parameters.
  • Engineered feature representations encoding target-projectile charge distributions, center-of-mass kinematics, and penetrability factors.
  • Achieved high-fidelity generalization in low-energy tail predictions benchmarked against standard thermonuclear reaction evaluations.

2. Quantum Neural Networks (QNN) for Nuclear Angular Differential Cross Sections

Quantum Machine Learning • Variational Circuits • Nuclear Dynamics

Investigating the representational capacity of Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) for non-linear scattering kinematics. Using benchmark experimental literature (PRC 114), this study analyzes whether variational quantum states offer superior inductive biases for highly oscillatory angular differential distributions compared to classical deep MLPs.

  • Derived laboratory-to-center-of-mass kinematic transformations and discretized scattering angular vectors into state-encoded quantum registers.
  • Trained variational circuits utilizing angle-embedding layers and parameterized entangling blocks.
  • Systematically evaluated forward-angle diffraction peaks and backward-angle interference fringes against classical baselines.

Figure 2: Comparative angular differential cross-section predictions: Quantum Neural Network vs. Classical baseline model.

3. Automated Extraction & Structure Analysis of Nuclear Hamiltonians

Scientific Literature Mining • Matrix Extraction • Open Science

Modern nuclear shell-model calculations depend on accurate two-body and three-body matrix elements (such as YL3N and GSMCC formulations). This work implements an end-to-end algorithmic pipeline that parses publication PDFs, extracts tabulated interaction coefficients, verifies matrix symmetry, and compiles reproducible, open-access databases.

Figure 3: Matrix element distribution and interaction spectral profiles compiled via algorithmic extraction.

4. Frequency-Domain Representation for Synthetic Media Forensics

Computer Vision • Media Forensics • Deep Learning

Investigating subtle structural and spectral inconsistencies in generative synthetic imagery. Developed detection frameworks that couple spatial convolutional feature extraction with discrete Fourier and DCT spectral transformations to identify upsampling artifacts characteristic of generative models.
