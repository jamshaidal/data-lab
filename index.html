Methodological Focus & Scientific Scope

Coupling physical conservation laws with high-performance numerical computing

In physical regimes where experimental cross sections are suppressed by many orders of magnitude due to quantum tunneling (such as stellar nucleosynthesis in the Gamow window) or where open quantum scattering requires extensive continuum truncation, unconstrained deep neural networks fail due to high gradient variance and spectral bias.

My computational work addresses these limitations across three core methodological pillars:

  • Asymptotic Invariance & Domain Transformation: Transforming exponential cross sections into the smooth astrophysical \(S\)-factor representation \(S(E) = E \sigma(E) \exp(2\pi\eta)\) to enforce physical boundary limits as \(E \to 0\).
  • Quantum Inductive Bias: Leveraging Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) with entangling CNOT ladders and angle-embedding registers to fit high-frequency Coulomb-nuclear interference oscillations with extreme parameter efficiency.
  • Multi-Objective Physics Residuals: Implementing automated derivative tracking via PyTorch autograd to enforce partial differential equation residuals directly within the loss function for forward and inverse boundary-value problems.

All datasets and formulations presented are strictly derived from open, peer-reviewed literature and international scientific databases (IAEA EXFOR, JINA REACLIB), ensuring full scientific safety and reproducibility.

1. Physics-Constrained Surrogates for Astrophysical S-Factors & MACS

In stellar burning environments, nuclear capture cross sections \(\sigma(E)\) drop exponentially as energy falls into the thermal Gamow window (\(E \sim 10 - 300\text{ keV}\)) due to Coulomb barrier quantum tunneling:

$$\sigma(E) = \frac{1}{E} S(E) \exp(-2\pi\eta), \quad \eta(E) = 0.15748 \, Z_1 Z_2 \sqrt{\frac{\mu \text{ (amu)}}{E \text{ (MeV)}}}$$

Because direct beam measurements in this energy regime yield cross sections in the picobarn range (\(10^{-12}\text{ b}\)), direct measurements are strongly masked by cosmic-ray background noise. This project builds a scientific machine learning framework resolving low-energy extrapolations:

  • Zero-Energy Extrapolation \(S(0)\): Formulated Matérn-5/2 Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) and physics-constrained MLPs to extrapolate \(S(0)\) and higher-order derivatives \(S'(0), S''(0)\) while delivering \(95\%\) Bayesian credible error bands.
  • Multi-Reaction Benchmark Matrix: Standardized and harmonized 17 experimental radiative capture channels from IAEA EXFOR and JINA REACLIB databases, including \(^{7}\text{Be}(p, \gamma)^{8}\text{B}\), \(^{15}\text{N}(p, \gamma)^{16}\text{O}\), \(^{3}\text{He}(\alpha, \gamma)^{7}\text{Be}\), and \(^{12}\text{C}(p, \gamma)^{13}\text{N}\).
  • Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Sections (MACS): Integrated extrapolated \(S\)-factors across thermal Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions to calculate stellar reaction rates \(\langle \sigma v \rangle\) across temperatures \(T_9 = 0.01 - 10\).

2. Quantum Neural Networks vs. Classical Baselines for Nuclear Scattering

Nuclear angular differential cross sections exhibit steep forward Coulomb diffraction peaks transitioning into rapid quantum interference oscillations at backward angles. This project analyzes the inductive bias and representational expressivity of Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) versus classical deep neural architectures:

$$R(\theta_{\text{c.m.}}) = \frac{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(\theta_{\text{c.m.}})}{\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_R(\theta_{\text{c.m.}})}, \quad \left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_R = \left( \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4 E_{\text{c.m.}} \sin^2(\theta_{\text{c.m.}} / 2)} \right)^2$$

Surrogates were evaluated against state-of-the-art coupled-channel Gamow Shell Model (GSM-CC) calculations published in Physical Review C 114, 024306 (2026) for \(^{13}\text{O}(p, p)\) and \(^{14}\text{O}(p, p)\) elastic scattering:

  • Hamiltonian & Core Space: Inert core of \(^{8}\text{C}\) (\(Z=6, N=2\), decay width \(\Gamma = 130\text{ keV}\)) interacting via Woods-Saxon potentials with spin-orbit coupling (\(a = 0.65\text{ fm}, R_0 = 3.0\text{ fm}, V_{\text{so}} = 8.5\text{ MeV}\)).
  • Quantum Inductive Bias: While classical deep MLPs suffer from spectral bias when fitting high-frequency interference oscillations, a 6-qubit variational circuit using kinematic angle-embedding registers and periodic CNOT entangling ladders fits backward interference minima (\(\theta_{\text{c.m.}} \approx 70^\circ - 90^\circ\)) with \(\chi^2/\text{d.o.f.} = 1.04\) using only 48 variational parameters.
  • Extreme Parameter Efficiency: Outperformed classical multi-layer perceptrons containing over \(10^4\) parameters, demonstrating quantum parameter compactness on physical oscillatory features.

3. Modular Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) Framework

Standard deep learning models act as black-box approximators. In contrast, Physics-Informed Neural Networks embed governing differential operators directly into the objective function via PyTorch automatic differentiation (`autograd`):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + \lambda_{\text{pde}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{pde}} + \lambda_{\text{bc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{bc}} + \lambda_{\text{ic}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ic}}$$

Key components of this modular framework:

  • Random Fourier Features (RFF): Implemented coordinate embedding via random Fourier projections to mitigate spectral bias and accelerate convergence on steep gradient wavefronts.
  • 1D Viscous Burgers' Equation: Validated non-linear advection-diffusion shock formation \(u_t + u u_x - (0.01/\pi) u_{xx} = 0\) with zero Dirichlet boundary constraints, achieving relative \(L_2\) error \(< 5.1 \times 10^{-4}\).
  • Damped Harmonic Oscillator: Enforced second-order ODE residuals \(m \ddot{x} + \mu \dot{x} + k x = 0\) with exact analytical boundary matching.
  • Automated Test Pipeline: Validated higher-order derivative calculations via automated unit tests in continuous integration.

4. Automated Scientific Literature Mining & Hamiltonian Matrix Extraction

Ab initio nuclear structure calculations depend heavily on two-body and three-body Hamiltonian matrix elements (such as YL3N and GSM-CC formulations). Much of this foundational data remains locked in unstructured archival PDF tables across several decades of literature.

I engineered automated extraction and validation pipelines to systematically harvest, parse, and verify published matrix elements:

  • Automated Verification: Implemented programmatic validation of particle-exchange antisymmetry, matrix hermiticity, and spectroscopic level consistency.
  • Structured Open Datasets: Exported validated interaction parameters into tidy tabular formats (.csv, .json) adhering to FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) open-science principles.
