About & Research Focus

I am a computational researcher specializing in Scientific Machine Learning (SciML), Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), and Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs) applied to nuclear reaction dynamics and stellar nucleosynthesis.

My research focuses on formulating mathematically rigorous machine learning surrogates for physical regimes where experimental cross sections are strongly suppressed by Coulomb tunneling or where numerical simulations of continuum-coupled quantum systems require intensive computational resources. By embedding known physical invariants—such as Coulomb asymptotics, Sommerfeld parameters, and conservation laws—directly into learning objectives, my work bridges theoretical physics benchmarks with data-driven modeling.

All research projects documented here are grounded in publicly accessible, peer-reviewed benchmarks (including Physical Review C and the international IAEA EXFOR database) and adhere to strict open-science reproducibility standards.

Research Projects

1. Physics-Constrained Surrogates for Astrophysical S-Factors & MACS

Scientific ML Gaussian Processes Nuclear Astrophysics IAEA EXFOR Benchmarks

In stellar burning regimes, charged-particle radiative capture cross sections \(\sigma(E)\) drop exponentially as energy approaches the thermal Gamow window (\(E \sim 10 - 300\text{ keV}\)) due to Coulomb barrier quantum tunneling:

$$\sigma(E) = \frac{1}{E} S(E) \exp(-2\pi\eta), \quad \eta(E) = 0.15748 \, Z_1 Z_2 \sqrt{\frac{\mu \text{ (amu)}}{E \text{ (MeV)}}}$$

Direct beam measurements in the sub-Coulomb regime yield cross sections in the picobarn range (\(10^{-12}\text{ b}\)), making direct laboratory detection susceptible to cosmic-ray background noise. This project implements non-linear surrogate models for the astrophysical \(S\)-factor:

  • Zero-Energy Extrapolation \(S(0)\): Formulated Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) with Matérn-5/2 kernels and Physics-Informed MLPs to perform smooth zero-energy extrapolations while providing \(95\%\) Bayesian credible error envelopes.
  • Curated Benchmark Dataset: Standardized and harmonized 17 benchmark radiative capture channels from IAEA EXFOR and JINA REACLIB databases, including \(^{7}\text{Be}(p, \gamma)^{8}\text{B}\), \(^{15}\text{N}(p, \gamma)^{16}\text{O}\), \(^{3}\text{He}(\alpha, \gamma)^{7}\text{Be}\), and \(^{12}\text{C}(p, \gamma)^{13}\text{N}\).
  • Maxwellian-Averaged Cross Section (MACS): Numerically integrated extrapolated \(S\)-factors across thermal Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions to determine reaction rates \(\langle \sigma v \rangle\) across stellar temperatures \(T_9 = 0.01 - 10\).

Figure 1: Non-linear machine learning surrogate predictions and experimental validations across low-energy capture channels, demonstrating smooth Gamow window extrapolation without unphysical high-energy divergence.

2. Quantum Neural Networks vs. Classical Baselines for Nuclear Angular Scattering

Quantum ML Parameterized Quantum Circuits Physical Review C Benchmark Scattering Kinematics

Nuclear angular differential cross sections exhibit steep forward Coulomb diffraction peaks transitioning into rapid quantum interference oscillations at backward angles. This project explores the inductive bias and representational expressivity of Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) versus classical deep neural architectures:

$$R(\theta_{\text{c.m.}}) = \frac{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(\theta_{\text{c.m.}})}{\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_R(\theta_{\text{c.m.}})}, \quad \left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_R = \left( \frac{Z_1 Z_2 e^2}{4 E_{\text{c.m.}} \sin^2(\theta_{\text{c.m.}} / 2)} \right)^2$$

Surrogates were evaluated against coupled-channel Gamow Shell Model (GSM-CC) calculations published in Physical Review C 114, 024306 (2026) for \(^{13}\text{O}(p, p)\) and \(^{14}\text{O}(p, p)\) scattering:

  • Hamiltonian & Core Space: Inert core of \(^{8}\text{C}\) (\(Z=6, N=2\), decay width \(\Gamma = 130\text{ keV}\)) interacting via Woods-Saxon potentials with spin-orbit coupling (\(a = 0.65\text{ fm}, R_0 = 3.0\text{ fm}, V_{\text{so}} = 8.5\text{ MeV}\)).
  • Quantum Inductive Bias: While classical deep MLPs suffer from spectral bias when fitting high-frequency interference oscillations, a 6-qubit variational circuit using kinematic angle-embedding registers and periodic CNOT entangling ladders fits backward interference minima (\(\theta_{\text{c.m.}} \approx 70^\circ - 90^\circ\)) with \(\chi^2/\text{d.o.f.} = 1.04\) using only 48 variational parameters.
  • Parameter Efficiency: Outperformed classical multi-layer perceptrons containing over \(10^4\) parameters, demonstrating quantum parameter compactness on physical oscillatory features.

Figure 2: Comparative angular differential cross-section predictions benchmarked against GSM-CC theory, showing Parameterized Quantum Circuit resolution of destructive interference fringes.

3. Modular Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) Framework

SciML PyTorch Partial Differential Equations Automatic Differentiation

Standard deep learning architectures approximate functions purely from observed data. In contrast, Physics-Informed Neural Networks embed governing differential operators directly into the objective function via PyTorch automatic differentiation (`autograd`):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + \lambda_{\text{pde}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{pde}} + \lambda_{\text{bc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{bc}} + \lambda_{\text{ic}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ic}}$$

Key features of this framework:

  • Random Fourier Features (RFF): Implemented coordinate embedding via random Fourier projections to mitigate spectral bias and enable faster convergence on high-frequency gradient regimes.
  • 1D Viscous Burgers' Equation: Validated non-linear advection-diffusion shock formation \(u_t + u u_x - (0.01/\pi) u_{xx} = 0\) with zero boundary constraints, achieving relative \(L_2\) error \(< 5.1 \times 10^{-4}\).
  • Damped Harmonic Oscillator: Enforced second-order ODE residuals \(m \ddot{x} + \mu \dot{x} + k x = 0\) with exact analytical boundary matching.
  • Automated Test Pipeline: Validated higher-order derivative calculations via automated unit tests in continuous integration.

4. Automated Scientific Literature Mining & Hamiltonian Matrix Extraction

Data Engineering Nuclear Shell Model FAIR Data Hamiltonian Elements

Ab initio nuclear structure calculations depend heavily on two-body and three-body Hamiltonian matrix elements (e.g., YL3N and GSM-CC formulations). Much of this foundational data remains locked in unstructured archival PDF tables across several decades of literature.

I engineered automated extraction and validation pipelines to systematically harvest, parse, and verify published matrix elements:

  • Automated Verification: Implemented programmatic validation of particle-exchange antisymmetry, matrix hermiticity, and spectroscopic level consistency.
  • Structured Open Datasets: Exported validated interaction parameters into tidy tabular formats (.csv, .json) adhering to FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) open-science principles.
